Module 1: The Quantitative Ecosystem & Business Models

Unlike discretionary management—where positions are governed by qualitative committee meetings—systematic asset managers operate via disciplined, data-driven algorithms backed by refutable economic hypotheses and empirical testing.

1.1 Strategic Paradigms Overview

Strategy Core Economic Thesis Investment Horizon Core Competencies
Factor Investing Persistent systematic risk premia (Momentum, Value, Quality, Low Vol). Medium to Long Term (Monthly) Cross-sectional econometrics, accounting ratios, portfolio optimization.
Statistical Arbitrage (StatArb) Mean-reverting temporary deviations in cointegrated pairs or baskets. Intraday to Multi-Day Cointegration tests (Johansen), Kalman Filters, market microstructure.
Trend Following (CTAs) Directional persistence driven by institutional hedging flows. Medium Term (Weeks to Months) Non-linear time series, dynamic leverage constraints, drawdown control.

  • Quant Developer: Engineers low-latency data pipelines, vectorized and event-driven backtesters, and broker/exchange execution APIs.
  • Risk Manager: Conducts scenario stress tests, calculates coherent tail metrics (VaR/CVaR), and enforces sector concentration boundaries.

Module 2: Critical Modeling Pitfalls & Anti-Bias Protocols

In quantitative modeling, the primary danger is rarely the absence of profitable signals; it is the abundance of illusory signals generated by methodological flaws in the research pipeline.

⚠️ The Three Biases That Destroy Production Strategies
  1. Look-Ahead Bias: Inadvertently incorporating information from time t+1 into decisions executed at time t (e.g., using quarterly earnings before their SEC/CVM filing timestamp).
  2. Survivorship Bias: Testing strategies exclusively on currently active equities, thereby ignoring delisted, distressed, or liquidated firms.
  3. Frictional Disregard: Neglecting bid-ask spread expansion, non-linear market impact, exchange clearing fees, and short-selling borrow rates (locate fees).

2.1 The Institutional Cross-Validation Protocol: Purged K-Fold

Standard randomized K-Fold cross-validation is strictly invalid in financial time series due to temporal autocorrelation and overlapping multi-period return windows.

The institutional standard requires:

  • Purging: Removing training observations whose evaluation horizons overlap with the test fold.
  • Embargo: Imposing a post-test quarantine window to neutralize residual memory and autoregressive shocks.

Module 3: Technical Interview Blueprint & GitHub Engineering

Hiring committees and portfolio managers evaluate dozens of code submissions weekly. A clean, modular GitHub repository is the single most decisive credential for passing technical screenings.

3.1 Portfolio Engineering: Dos and Don'ts

❌ Disqualifying Submission Patterns ✅ Institutional Portfolio Hallmarks
Stock price forecasting using black-box LSTMs without friction costs. Clean ETL pipelines with explicit point-in-time publication timestamps.
Monolithic, un-tested Jupyter Notebooks with hundreds of global variables. Modular Python packages with structured classes and pytest unit tests.
Reporting Sharpe ratio alone without friction sensitivity. Full distribution analytics: Sortino, Calmar, Max Drawdown, and Turnover.

3.2 Core Econometric Interview Question

📌 Interview Concept: Cross-Sectional Momentum Lag
Question: Why does the standard academic cross-sectional Momentum factor (Jegadeesh & Titman) evaluate returns from months t-12 to t-2, deliberately skipping month t-1?
Answer: Month t-1 is skipped to isolate persistent intermediate-term trend while stripping out short-term mean-reversion caused by temporary liquidity shocks and bid-ask bounce.
